# Configuration and technical review procedure

The technical release candidate is the exact source tree and host executable in
`binary-identity.json`, with the nine declared suite inputs and dependency lock
in `tests/manifest.json`. `SHA256SUMS` covers this dossier's retained records.
The executable remains in the identified Actions artifact; the committed record
is an inventory/hash binding, not a replacement compiler distribution.

Before using a new baseline, retain its executable, raw hosted job log, evidence
bundle and identity; verify all hashes; enumerate every operational requirement
and actual outcome; compare source/configuration changes; and reassess all
anomalies and restrictions. Do not carry a previous binary's result onto a newer
compiler revision. Keep historical red/green records at their own source identity.

Technical review must inspect the actual named outcomes, source/test meaning,
compiler-to-query assumptions, negative controls, manifest source-label limit,
source/binary consistency and profile restrictions. Record reviewer identity,
competence, scope, findings, resolutions and review date in a separate controlled
record. A Codex peer review can identify technical defects; it does not establish
regulatory independence, project safety acceptance or assessor approval.

Change an intake control to `reviewed` only after the named reviewer actually
accepts its evidence under the consuming project's process. Never populate
reviewer or approval fields with a future role to make the checker green.
Unresolved project classification, safety plans and assessment objectives remain
pending in this reusable dossier. A project may narrow its use and retain justified
restrictions; it must not silently remove an anomaly to satisfy the checker.
